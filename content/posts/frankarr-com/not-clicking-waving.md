I’ve been helping my kids with their homework, and one of them (I wont name who, so as to avoid any embarrassment) had to deliver a presentation in PowerPoint. I wanted to add a bit of pizzazz to the experience looked at hooking up a [Kinect](http://www.xbox.com/en-US/kinect) so that they could present with gestures, rather than clicking with a mouse.

Seems like a good idea, right?

I grabbed the [Kinect for Windows SDK](http://research.microsoft.com/en-us/um/redmond/projects/kinectsdk/download.aspx), and went looking on [codeplex](http://www.codeplex.com/) for [kinect related samples](http://www.codeplex.com/site/search?query=kinect&ac=8), where I found the [Kinect PowerPoint Control](http://kinectpowerpoint.codeplex.com/). It was nice, but It didn’t do exactly what I wanted.

By coincidence, [Dr Neil](http://www.roodyn.com/) sent me an email about [nsquared slyda](http://nsquaredsolutions.com/slyda/), which just lets me change between slides using hand gestures.

[](http://nsquaredsolutions.com/slyda/)

Perfecto!!

We had a practice session and kiddo can move forward and back, with a wave of the hands.

Next stop, the classroom!!!

BTW – I see that boffins at [nsquared solutions](http://nsquaredsolutions.com/) are working on more Kinect apps. I got a ping last night from [Dr Neil](http://roodyn.com/) about [nsquared spydar,](http://nsquaredsolutions.com/spydar/) which is like the [Coding4Fun Kinect Turret](http://kinectturret.codeplex.com/) minus the violence.  I got it set up – now let’s see who comes and uses my PC when I’m away….
